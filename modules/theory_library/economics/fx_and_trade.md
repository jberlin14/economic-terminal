# FX and International Trade Theory

## HIGH-LEVEL

Exchange rates are driven by interest rate differentials, growth differentials, trade flows, and risk appetite. When US rates are higher than peers, capital flows into the dollar (carry trade). When the US economy is stronger, the dollar strengthens. In risk-off episodes, the dollar strengthens as a safe haven.

The "impossible trinity" says you can only have two of three: free capital flows, independent monetary policy, and a fixed exchange rate. The US has free capital and independent policy, so the dollar floats. China manages its exchange rate but restricts capital flows.

A strong dollar hurts US exporters, tightens financial conditions for EM borrowers (USD-denominated debt), and is deflationary for imported goods. A weak dollar is the opposite — stimulative globally but can fuel commodity inflation.

## RIGOROUS

### Mundell-Fleming Model
Extends IS-LM to an open economy. Key predictions under floating exchange rates:

- **Expansionary monetary policy** (rate cuts): Lower rates → capital outflows → currency depreciation → exports become competitive → GDP rises through both lower rates AND weaker currency (reinforcing channels)
- **Expansionary fiscal policy** (deficit spending): Higher growth → higher rates (crowding out) → capital inflows → currency appreciation → exports fall → partial offset of fiscal stimulus (the "twin deficit" problem)

Implication for US policy mix: aggressive fiscal expansion + tight monetary policy = strong dollar + wide trade deficit. This is roughly the 2023-2025 configuration.

### Interest Rate Parity
**Covered interest rate parity (CIP)**: Forward FX rates eliminate arbitrage between investing domestically vs. investing abroad and hedging. The forward discount/premium = interest rate differential.

**Uncovered interest rate parity (UIP)**: Higher-yielding currencies should depreciate to eliminate excess returns. In practice, UIP is systematically violated — high-yield currencies tend to APPRECIATE in the short run (carry trade profitability). UIP holds in the long run through periodic sharp reversals ("carry trade unwinds" during risk-off episodes).

This violation creates the carry trade: borrow in low-yield currencies (JPY, CHF), invest in high-yield currencies (MXN, BRL, AUD). Profitable in calm markets, catastrophic during risk-off events when everything unwinds simultaneously.

### Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
The long-run anchor for exchange rates. A currency is "overvalued" if goods are more expensive in that country than abroad (after currency conversion). PPP suggests exchange rates should adjust to equalize purchasing power across countries.

PPP holds poorly in the short run (5 years or less) but reasonably well in the very long run (10-20 years). Deviations can persist for extended periods due to:
- Balassa-Samuelson effect (productivity-driven real appreciation in developing countries)
- Capital flows overwhelming trade flows
- Policy intervention (managed exchange rates)

### Dollar Cycle Theory (Dollar Smile)
The dollar tends to strengthen at two extremes:
1. **US economy much stronger than rest of world**: Growth differential attracts capital (right side of smile)
2. **Global risk-off / crisis**: Safe-haven demand for USD and US Treasuries (left side of smile)
3. **Dollar weakens in the middle**: When global growth is synchronized and risk appetite is high, capital flows OUT of the US toward higher-yielding opportunities abroad

### J-Curve Effect
After a currency depreciation, the trade balance initially WORSENS before improving:
- Short run (0-6 months): Import prices rise immediately, but export/import volumes adjust slowly (contracts locked in, supply chain rigidity). Higher import costs with unchanged volumes → wider deficit.
- Medium run (6-18 months): Exporters gain market share, importers substitute domestically. Volume effects dominate price effects → trade balance improves.

Policy implication: tariffs and currency depreciation don't immediately fix trade deficits. The short-run pain precedes the medium-run adjustment, creating political pressure to abandon the policy prematurely.

### EM Currency Dynamics
EM currencies face additional pressures not present in DM:
- **Original sin**: EM countries borrow in USD because investors don't trust local currency debt. Dollar strengthening → EM debt service costs rise → capital flight → further depreciation → vicious cycle.
- **Sudden stops**: Capital inflows reverse abruptly when global risk appetite shifts. EM countries with current account deficits and low reserves are most vulnerable.
- **Commodity dependence**: Many EM currencies are correlated with commodity prices (AUD/iron ore, BRL/soybeans, MXN/oil+remittances, ZAR/gold-platinum). Commodity price collapse → currency crisis.
- **Carry trade vulnerability**: High-yielding EM currencies attract carry flows that mask fundamental weaknesses. When carry unwinds, the depreciation is violent.

### FX as a Risk Barometer
- **USD/JPY**: Risk-on/risk-off proxy. Yen strengthens in risk-off (carry trade unwind) and weakens in risk-on. Also sensitive to BOJ/Fed rate differential.
- **EUR/USD**: The "anti-dollar." Reflects US vs. Eurozone growth/rate differentials. Also a proxy for global risk appetite (EUR strengthens when global growth broadens).
- **USD/MXN**: EM risk proxy for US-linked EM. Sensitive to trade policy, nearshoring narrative, Banxico rate decisions, and political risk.
- **DXY (Dollar Index)**: Weighted basket (57% EUR, 14% JPY, 12% GBP, etc.). Broad USD direction but heavily EUR-dependent.

### Incentive Structure
- **Surplus countries** (China, Germany, Japan) have incentives to keep currencies weak to support export sectors. Intervention, reserve accumulation, and capital controls serve this purpose. Trade partners object (currency manipulation accusations).
- **Deficit countries** (US, UK) have incentives for currency strength (cheaper imports, lower inflation) but face competitiveness loss. The "exorbitant privilege" of USD reserve status allows the US to run persistent deficits financed by foreign demand for Treasuries.
- **EM central banks** face a trilemma between supporting growth (low rates), fighting inflation (high rates), and defending the currency (high rates). The optimal choice depends on which risk is most acute.
- **Carry traders** provide liquidity and capital flows that can stabilize EM currencies in calm periods but amplify moves in stressed periods. Their incentive is pure yield — they don't care about fundamentals until fundamentals force a re-evaluation.
